Trust Model Explanation
=======================

Purpose
-------
This document explains what "trust" means in the project, how it is used,
and how it affects secure V2X operation.

What Trust Means Here
---------------------
In this project, trust is an operational score used to guide security and
handover decisions.

It is not presented as:
- a formal reputation proof
- a human trust concept
- a universal security guarantee

Instead, trust is used as a practical control signal inside the simulated
V2X framework.

Role of Trust in the Framework
------------------------------
Trust connects packet-level observations with higher-level control behavior.

It helps answer questions such as:
- Should this vehicle be treated as reliable?
- Should the next RSU handover use FAST or FULL authentication?
- Should the vehicle be denied when its behavior looks unsafe?

Trust Evidence Sources
----------------------
Trust is influenced by observed behavior in the system, including:

- successful validated communication behavior
- replay-related suspicious behavior
- tampered / invalid-signature style behavior
- malicious or suspicious reporting patterns
- infrastructure-side trust context when enabled
- freshness of previously stored trust information

This means trust is not static.
It changes according to observed communication behavior and decision history.

Trust as Decision Input
-----------------------
The trust score is mainly used during RSU handover.

The framework applies three outcomes:

- FAST authentication
- FULL authentication
- REJECT

Typical interpretation:
- high and fresh trust -> FAST
- moderate or stale trust -> FULL
- low trust -> REJECT

Trust Freshness
---------------
Trust is not used blindly.

Even if a vehicle had a good previous trust score, that score may become less
useful if it is too old.

For this reason, the framework also considers trust freshness or staleness.

Meaning:
- fresh trust can support quick handover
- stale trust should trigger stronger checking
- outdated trust should not automatically justify FAST authentication

Why Trust Is Important
----------------------
Packet validation alone is not enough in a dynamic V2X setting.

A packet may be syntactically valid but still come from a vehicle whose recent
behavior is suspicious.

Trust therefore adds a second layer of protection:
- packet-level filtering handles clearly invalid traffic
- trust-level control handles uncertain or suspicious behavior over time

Relation to Blockchain Support
------------------------------
When blockchain-assisted trust continuity is enabled, trust can be supported
across RSU regions through commit/query behavior and local cache support.

This does not mean the blockchain makes the system automatically secure.

Its role is narrower:
- preserve trust continuity
- reduce repeated re-learning across RSUs
- support continuity when local trust is missing or stale

Relation to Privacy
-------------------
Privacy mechanisms such as pseudonym rotation may reduce linkability, but they
can also complicate trust continuity.

This is why trust and privacy are evaluated together in the project rather than
as isolated features.

Safe Paper Wording
------------------
In this work, trust is used as an operational decision variable derived from
observed communication behavior and freshness conditions. It guides handover
control by selecting FAST, FULL, or REJECT behavior rather than serving as a
standalone formal proof of security.
